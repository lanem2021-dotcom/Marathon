Strength & Mobility

Two sessions a week, layered onto your run days. About 20 minutes each, done after the run — never before.

Strength A — Tuesdays

3×12 single-leg glute bridges/side, 3×10 calf raises (slow eccentric), 3×10 clamshells/side, 2×30s side plank/side.

Strength B — Thursdays

3×10 Bulgarian split squats/side, 3×12 standing band hip abductions/side, 3×15 single-leg calf raises/side, 2×45s plank.

Mobility — Sundays, after your long run: 10 minutes of ankle circles, resistance band inversion/eversion, calf and hip flexor stretching, and foam rolling the IT band and calves. Targets the left ankle pronation directly.

Tips & Gear

Shoes & Ankle

Get a gait analysis at a specialty running store. Recommended: ASICS GT-2000, Brooks Adrenaline GTS, New Balance 860, or Saucony Guide — medial support without being overly corrective.

Rotate two pairs once you're in the build phase; alternating extends foam life and reduces repetitive stress.

Easy Pace Rule

80% of runs should be easy — full sentences without gasping. Tempo pace is comfortably hard: a few words at a time, not full sentences.

Tennis + Running

Treat a hard tennis session like a workout day. Don't play a hard match and run long the same day — ankles and calves need 24–48 hours between high-impact sessions.

Warning Signs

Inside ankle pain (posterior tibial tendinitis) is the classic pronation injury — back off immediately, ice, rest 2–3 days. IT band pain: foam roll IT band, glutes, TFL. Shin splints: cut mileage 25%, softer surfaces, check shoe wear.

Race Day

Nothing new on race day — not shoes, socks, nutrition, or clothing. Start slow; crowd energy pulls you out faster than planned. Break the race into thirds: miles 1–9 conservative, 10–18 steady, 19–26 give what you have left.
